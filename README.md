# Breast-Cancer-Decision-Support-Systems-with-SHAP-and-LIME
Breast cancer, defined as abnormalities in the epithelial tissues of the breast which invades the stroma, mammary ducts, or lobes (Hameed, et al., 2022; Shrma et al., 2010), stands as one of the most widespread forms of cancer globally, affecting millions of individuals annually, particularly women (Wilkinson & Gathani, 2022; Czeczelewski et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2023). Reports from the World Cancer Research (WCR, 2022) and the NHS (2019) emphasise its disproportionate impact on women, and the World Health Organisation (WHO, 2023) reports that in 2020 alone, breast cancer afflicted 2.3 million women worldwide, leading to 685,000 fatalities. Alarmingly, the statistics from Breast Cancer UK (2020) reveal approximately 55,545 new cases of breast cancer in women each year, translating to over 150 cases daily. In 2019, breast Cancer UK (2020) reported 25,100 cases of breast cancer in men, resulting in 12,100 fatalities worldwide. This aliment knows no bounds in terms of age, affecting women post-puberty and prevalent in all nations. Chowdhury (2020) highlights its occurrence across age groups, with rates escalating notably in older demographics. Breast Cancer UK (2020) underscores this point, revealing that 18% of breast cancer cases occur in women under 50, while the majority, 82%, manifest in those aged 50 or older. This malignant neoplasm pose the greatest global burden for women, accounting for an estimated 107.8 million Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs), with breast cancer contributing 19.6 million DALYs (Sung et al., 2020; Zang et al., 2023). In the United States, breast cancer is expected to make up 29% of all new cancers in women. Data from 2018 GLOBOCAN indicates that the age-standardised incidence rates (ASIR) of breast cancer correlate strongly and positively with the Human Development Index (HDI). ASIR was highest in countries with very high HDI (75.6 per 100,000) and more than 200% lower in medium and low HDI countries (27.8 per 100,000 and 36.1 per 100,000, respectively) according to 2020 data (Harbeck & Grant, 2017).

Breast cancer incidence and mortality rates have risen in the past three decades. Between 1990 and 2016, breast cancer incidence more than doubled in 60 out of 102 countries, including Afghanistan, the Philippines, Brazil, and Argentina, while deaths doubled in 43 out of 102 countries, such as Yemen, Paraguay, Libya, and Saudi Arabia (Zhang et al., 2023). Projections suggest that by 2030, the global number of new cases annually will reach 2.7 million, with 0.87 million deaths. In low- and medium-income countries, breast cancer incidence is expected to continue increasing due to lifestyle changes such as delayed pregnancies, reduced breastfeeding, early onset of menstruation, lack of physical activity, poor diet, and detection methods (Zhang et al., 2023).
